Spectrum — overtone scope
Fundamental — Hz Note — quiet loud

Chips sit on the spectrogram at each harmonic. Click one to mute it (a narrow notch filter), or switch to solo mode to hear that harmonic alone. Pitch tracking runs on the pre-EQ tap using the tuner's MPM detector, so muting never confuses the tracker. Lock pitch freezes the current fundamental when tracking gets jumpy.

The filter chain is connected when you open this tab and disconnected when you leave, so nothing here can alter your tone on the Preamp tab. Mute and solo choices persist and are restored on return.

Time-domain WSOLA: the signal is time-stretched by the pitch ratio with a correlation-matched splice, then resampled back to real time. Clean on single notes, increasingly phasey on chords — a phase vocoder would do better on polyphony but costs far more CPU than a Pi Zero has.

Expect about 49 ms of added latency on the AudioWorklet host, or about 130 ms on the ScriptProcessor fallback. That is noticeable when playing. It is set by the correlation search, which must span a full period of the lowest note you want to track — shortening it does not make the shifter tighter, it makes it stop shifting.

Two hosts run the same DSP. Served over http/https you get the AudioWorklet, which runs on the audio thread. Opened as a plain file you get a ScriptProcessor on the main thread — deprecated, higher latency, and it can break up while the analyzer is drawing, but it needs no server.

The shifter sits first in the chain, ahead of the drive section, the way a drop-tune pedal goes in front of a preamp. It stays engaged when you leave this tab, and the Preamp status line says so — nothing here is invisible. The tuner keeps reading the unshifted strings, so you tune the instrument, not the effect.
